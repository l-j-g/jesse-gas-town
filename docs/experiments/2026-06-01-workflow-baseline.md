## Hypothesis

A minimal 1h moving-average trend-following baseline can act as the control strategy for later MA crossover, three-MA, multi-timeframe, regime-filter, and conviction-allocation experiments.

## Simplest Testable Version

- 1h `Sandbox` / `BTC-USDT` route
- 20/50/100 SMA trend and crossover model
- ATR stop, fixed R-multiple target, 1x futures assumptions
- risk-based size capped by notional percentage
- no live exchange keys or live execution setup

## Results

The baseline ran end-to-end through Jesse's research backtest path and produced metrics, but it lost money on a synthetic regime path. This is not evidence of profitability and should not be optimized directly.

## Risks And Failure Modes

- Synthetic candles are not market data; they only verify mechanics.
- Only 9 trades: too few for strategy quality conclusions.
- All trades lost: reject the candidate as-is.
- Fees consumed a material part of the small losses, so fee sensitivity matters.
- Only long trades fired; short-side logic and downtrend behavior need separate validation.
- Jesse emitted database debug messages about `closedtrade.updated_at`, indicating local DB schema/migration drift even though the run exited successfully.
- Moving-average systems are regime dependent and can be damaged by chop, fees, and late exits.

## One Refinement

Use real imported BTC-USDT 1m candles and rerun the same 1h route before changing strategy logic.
